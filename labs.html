The Labs

Five worked examples you step through one operation at a time. Every number on screen is computed in your browser from a seeded starting point — nothing is typed in, so nothing can quietly be wrong.

Interactive 5 labs · no install, no account, runs offline
Why these exist

Reading "the attention weights are a softmax over scaled dot products" is not the same as watching a 4×4 matrix of scores turn into a 4×4 matrix of probabilities whose rows you can check sum to 1.

Each lab takes the smallest example that still shows the whole mechanism, then walks it. Shapes are labelled at every step. Where a derivation can be verified numerically, the lab verifies it on screen rather than asking you to take its word.

02How to get the most out of them

Predict before you advance. Every lab moves one primitive operation per step. Before clicking forward, say out loud what shape the next matrix will be. Getting that wrong is more informative than getting it right, and it is the specific skill an interviewer is probing when they ask you to "walk me through the forward pass".

Watch the shapes, not the values. The individual numbers are arbitrary — they come from a seeded random generator. The shapes are not. Almost every real confusion about attention is a confusion about which axis is which.

Take the self-check steps seriously. Several labs end a tab by verifying their own arithmetic: FlashAttention's tiled output compared against the naive one, analytic gradients compared against numerical ones, a softmax row summed. Those steps are the difference between a demo and a proof.

You have got what these are for when you can take a sequence length, a model dimension and a head count, and state the shape of every tensor between the input embedding and the attention output — without opening the lab.
